Daraxonrasib (RMC-6236), an oral KRAS inhibitor, is being hailed as the first meaningful treatment advance for metastatic pancreatic cancer in decades, with trial data showing survival of roughly 13 months versus about 6 months for chemotherapy in previously treated patients. Metastatic pancreatic cancer has had no meaningful new treatment for decades beyond variations of chemotherapy, so a first-in-class RAS inhibitor reaching approval is genuinely practice-changing for a disease with one of the lowest survival rates. The survival benefit is modest in absolute terms (~13 vs ~6 months), and trials enroll heavily pretreated late-stage patients, which makes the numbers look underwhelming to lay observers; daraxonrasib targets RAS pathway mutations that occur in more than 90% of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma tumors.

**Background**: KRAS is a small GTPase that acts as a molecular switch telling cells when to grow, cycling between active and inactive states; cancer-causing mutations lock it in the active state. KRAS was long considered 'undruggable' until G12C-specific inhibitors proved small molecules could target it, and daraxonrasib extends this approach to the broader set of RAS mutations common in pancreatic cancer.

A blogger discovered that routing Ethernet through a monitor's USB hub reduced download speeds by 75% because the added latency caused the Ethernet adapter's buffer to overflow and drop packets. Switching to a different adapter that required less frequent acknowledgements resolved the issue. This case highlights how opaque USB hub and Ethernet adapter interactions can silently degrade network performance, affecting users who rely on docking stations or monitor hubs for wired connectivity. It underscores the importance of understanding latency and buffer dynamics in USB-based networking. The root cause was increased latency from the monitor's USB hub, which prevented the laptop from acknowledging packets quickly enough, filling the adapter's buffer and causing packet drops. A different adapter with a different chipset that required less frequent acknowledgements fixed the problem.

**Background**: USB hubs, especially those integrated into monitors, can introduce additional latency compared to direct connections. Ethernet over USB relies on the host acknowledging packets; if latency is too high, the adapter's buffer may overflow, leading to dropped packets and reduced throughput. USB 2.0 has higher latency than USB 3.0, and hub quality can vary significantly.

## [Ghostscript CVE-2026-125396: Upload2Shell Sandbox Bypass](https://pwn.ai/upload2shell) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A new Ghostscript vulnerability, CVE-2026-125396, has been publicly disclosed, allowing a bypass of the -dSAFER sandbox via the .putgstringcopy and .forceput operators in versions before 10.08.0. The issue is detailed on pwn.ai under the name 'Upload2Shell' and has been assigned a CVSS 4.0 score of 8.5 (High). This vulnerability is significant because Ghostscript is widely used to process untrusted files (e.g., PDFs and PostScript) in web upload pipelines, email gateways, and document converters, so a sandbox bypass can lead to remote code execution. It underscores the broader risk of running interpreters on untrusted input without robust isolation. The flaw specifically abuses the .putgstringcopy and .forceput operators to escape the -dSAFER restrictions, and the CVSS 4.0 score of 8.5 reflects high impact but no known active exploitation (EPSS < 1%, not in KEV). Users should upgrade to Ghostscript 10.08.0 or later, and consider additional sandboxing (e.g., seccomp, Landlock) for defense in depth.

hackernews · f311a · Nov 21, 18:29 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50592097)

**Background**: Ghostscript is an interpreter for PostScript and PDF files, and its -dSAFER option is intended to prevent malicious documents from accessing the file system or executing commands. However, PostScript is a full programming language, and securely sandboxing it is notoriously difficult; past bypasses have led to high-profile compromises. CVE-2026-125396 is the latest in a series of sandbox escape vulnerabilities, highlighting the challenge of safely processing untrusted input.
